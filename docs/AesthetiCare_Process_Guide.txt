AESTHETICARE PROCESS GUIDE

1. CLINIC REGISTRATION AND OCR

Clinic owner registers
-> verifies email through OTP
-> enters clinic and owner details, branch address, and map pin
-> uploads required documents
-> submits registration
-> System Administrator reviews the application and OCR results
-> approve or reject
-> approved owner activates the account and chooses a plan

Detailed steps:
1. The applicant selects Register as Clinic Owner.
2. They provide personal details, clinic details, Philippine contact number,
   branch address, and map pin.
3. They verify their email through the registration OTP.
4. They upload the required registration documents and submit the application.
5. The application is placed in Pending Review. The applicant cannot use the
   clinic workspace yet.
6. In System Admin > Clinic Verification, an administrator opens the
   application and triggers OCR for each document.
7. OCR extracts readable text and returns a confidence score.
   - 85% or above: the document can be marked OCR-verified.
   - Below 85%: the document requires manual review.
   - OCR supports review; it does not replace the System Administrator's
     approval decision.
8. The System Administrator checks document validity, names, details, clinic
   address, and OCR output.
   - Approve: an activation email is sent. The owner activates the account,
     signs in, and selects/pays for Basic or Premium.
   - Reject: the applicant receives the reason and may correct their details
     or documents and register again using the same email.

For reliable OCR, documents should be upright, uncropped, sharp, well lit,
and without glare or reflections.


2. GETTING CLINIC SUPPLIES

Inventory request
-> Procurement verifies request and supplier catalog terms
-> Finance approves funding
-> Procurement issues purchase order
-> Supplier confirms or declines
-> Logistics receives and inspects goods
-> Inventory is updated
-> Finance records invoice/payment

Detailed steps:
1. Inventory identifies low stock or a needed supply.
2. Inventory creates a purchase request with items, quantities, and reason.
3. Procurement reviews the request. It confirms the supplier catalog item,
   quantity, availability, price, tax, discount, other charges, payment terms,
   delivery date, and delivery location.
4. Procurement may return or reject incomplete requests. Complete requests are
   sent to Finance for funding approval.
5. Finance approves, returns, or rejects the funding request.
   - Approve: the request goes back to Procurement for ordering.
   - Return: Procurement corrects and resubmits it.
   - Reject: no purchase order is issued.
6. Procurement issues the purchase order to the selected supplier.
7. The supplier confirms or declines the purchase order.
   - Declined: Procurement changes supplier, revises, or cancels the order.
   - Confirmed: the order becomes available to Logistics.
8. Logistics receives and inspects goods. It records accepted quantities,
   partial deliveries, damaged/rejected goods, and discrepancies.
9. Accepted quantities update Inventory stock.
10. Finance reviews the supplier invoice/payable and records payment.

Customer product orders are separate. They are pickup-only and do not enter
the supplier logistics workflow.

2A. HOW CLINIC SUPPLIES ARE PAID

The supply workflow controls and records supplier payments. It does not yet
initiate a real bank transfer or PayMongo payout to a supplier.

Supplier-confirmed PO
-> Logistics receives and onboards accepted goods
-> supplier submits invoice (or Finance records the manual invoice)
-> Finance performs three-way matching
-> Finance approves invoice for payment
-> Finance prepares, reviews, and approves payment record
-> Finance makes the external payment
-> Finance uploads proof and records settlement

Detailed steps:
1. Procurement includes the agreed payment terms in the funding request and
   purchase order. The supplier catalog remains the source for item price,
   VAT/tax treatment, discounts, and per-unit charges.
2. The supplier confirms the purchase order and Logistics receives/inspects
   the goods. Accepted goods must be onboarded before invoicing.
3. The supplier submits an invoice through the supplier flow, or Finance
   creates/records the supplier invoice for a manual supplier arrangement.
4. The invoice is initially Submitted. Finance attaches the invoice document
   and starts verification.
5. Finance performs a three-way match:
   - Purchase Order: what the clinic ordered.
   - Receiving/Inspection: what Logistics accepted into inventory.
   - Supplier Invoice: what the supplier is charging.
6. A mismatch creates a Disputed invoice. Finance resolves it before payment.
7. A matched invoice is approved for payment. The system requires separation
   of duties: the person who created or verified an invoice cannot approve it.
8. Finance creates a payment record, reviews it, and a different authorized
   Finance user approves it.
9. Finance changes the payment record to Processing, then makes the real
   payment outside AesthetiCare, such as through bank transfer or another
   approved external/electronic method.
10. After the external payment succeeds, Finance uploads payment proof,
    enters the payment reference, payment date, and method, then records the
    settlement as Paid.
11. The system updates the supplier invoice, procurement payable/financial
    record, reserved budget, actual spending, and purchase-order status.

Supported recorded payment methods are Manual/External and Electronic.
Therefore, the platform provides an auditable accounts-payable workflow, not
an automatic supplier-money-transfer integration.


3. BASE PAY, PAYROLL, FINANCE, AND EMPLOYEE PAYSLIPS

HR sets employee base pay
-> attendance and approved overtime are recorded
-> HR prepares payroll entries
-> HR submits monthly payroll summary
-> Finance approves or returns it
-> payroll is released
-> employee sees payslip

Detailed steps:
1. HR creates the employee account and assigns role/position.
2. HR sets the employee's Base Pay using the supported salary basis.
3. For each payroll period, the system uses base pay, completed attendance,
   approved overtime, deductions, adjustments, and payroll settings.
4. HR generates payroll entries and reviews the calculations.
5. HR submits the monthly payroll summary to Finance.
6. Finance reviews it.
   - Approve: the payroll is locked for release.
   - Return/reject: HR corrects the entries and resubmits them.
7. After release, the employee opens My Payslips to see the period, pay
   breakdown, deductions, overtime effects, net pay, and status.
8. The system records approval and release. It does not automatically transfer
   money to a bank account.

This full payroll workflow is Premium-only.


4. ATTENDANCE TAKING

HR/configured staff issues branch daily QR
-> employee opens My Attendance on mobile
-> employee permits camera and location
-> employee takes current photo proof
-> employee scans QR
-> system validates QR, branch, GPS/geofence, and scan order
-> time-in or time-out is recorded

Detailed steps:
1. The clinic configures the branch attendance location and allowed geofence.
2. An authorized HR/manager user opens Attendance > QR Station and generates
   or refreshes the branch QR code.
3. The employee opens My Attendance on a mobile device.
4. The employee permits camera and location, takes current photo proof, and
   scans the branch QR.
5. The system verifies that the QR is valid and unexpired, the employee belongs
   to the permitted branch, the device is inside the geofence, location
   accuracy is acceptable, and the scan order is valid.
6. The first valid scan records time-in. The next valid scan records time-out.
7. Duplicate and out-of-order scans are rejected.
8. HR can review records, inspect proof/details, and make audited corrections
   if their role permits it.

Face registration is available for employee identity setup. The daily
attendance flow is currently based on QR, GPS/geofence validation, and current
photo proof.


5. SHIFT CREATION AND ASSIGNMENT

HR creates shift template
-> HR assigns it to employee/date or recurring schedule
-> employee sees the assignment
-> attendance and overtime use the shift as reference

Detailed steps:
1. HR opens Add Shift.
2. HR defines the shift name, start/end time, break details when applicable,
   branch, and relevant days/dates.
3. HR saves the shift.
4. HR opens Shift Assignment.
5. HR selects the employee, branch, date or recurring schedule, and assigns
   the shift.
6. The employee sees the assignment in their workspace/dashboard.
7. Attendance is compared with the assigned shift for expected time-in/out,
   lateness, and overtime eligibility.
8. Overtime can be requested only when completed attendance extends beyond the
   assigned shift.

Shifts are Premium-only.


6. APPOINTMENT DATE AVAILABILITY: BASIC VS PREMIUM

Both Basic and Premium support customer booking availability.

BASIC
- One clinic branch.
- The clinic publishes recurring bookable days and times for each practitioner.
- Example: a practitioner can be bookable Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM.
- No HR shifts, attendance, leave, overtime, base pay, or payroll workflows.

PREMIUM
- Includes everything in Basic.
- Multiple clinic branches are supported.
- Availability can be configured per practitioner and branch.
- HR shifts, attendance, leave, overtime, and payroll are available.

IMPORTANT
- Booking Availability is separate from HR shifts.
- A shift does not automatically publish appointment slots to customers.
- In both plans, customers see only dates/times that are published as available,
  do not conflict with existing bookings, and satisfy service duration and
  resource checks.


7. CLINIC OWNER ACCOUNT CLOSURE AND EMPLOYEE EFFECTS

Clinic owner submits closure request
-> System Administrator reviews it
-> reject, deactivate owner only, transfer ownership, or approve full shutdown

The owner opens Account Settings > Account Access, selects a closure type,
acknowledges the consequences, types CLOSE MY ACCOUNT, and submits the request.
It is not immediate; a System Administrator must review it.

A. CLOSE OWNER ACCOUNT ONLY (DEACTIVATE)
1. The System Administrator approves the request.
2. The owner login becomes inactive.
3. Clinic branches remain active.
4. Employees linked to the clinic remain active and can continue using the
   pages/actions their role allows.
5. Clinic records, appointments, finance history, and audit logs are retained.
6. The owner can restore owner access by signing in within 30 days.

This option is appropriate only when another authorized clinic administrator
can continue managing the clinic.

B. TRANSFER MAIN BRANCH OWNERSHIP
1. The owner enters the email of an eligible existing clinic owner/admin.
2. The System Administrator approves the transfer.
3. Connected branch ownership is reassigned to that account.
4. The previous owner account is closed.
5. Employees remain active, so clinic operations continue under the new owner.

C. REQUEST FULL CLINIC SHUTDOWN
1. The System Administrator approves the request.
2. The owner account is closed.
3. Connected branches are marked inactive and archived.
4. Employees whose branch is one of the closed branches are archived/inactive.
5. Those employees can no longer use the employee portal until an authorized
   administrator restores them.
6. Clinic records are retained for audit, compliance, legal support, and
   continuity; they are not immediately erased.


8. CUSTOMER SERVICE APPOINTMENT PROCESS

Customer selects a clinic and service
-> selects an available date/time and practitioner
-> sends booking request
-> clinic approves or rejects
-> customer signs contract and pays required amount
-> assigned practitioner verifies service key at the appointment
-> service starts and is completed
-> customer confirms completion

Detailed steps:
1. The customer opens a clinic page, selects one or more published services,
   and selects an available date/time.
2. The system checks practitioner availability, service duration, booking
   conflicts, and required resources before accepting the request.
3. The customer submits the booking request.
4. Authorized clinic staff review it in Booking Requests.
   - Approve: the booking moves forward for contract/payment preparation.
   - Reject: the customer receives the decision and reason where provided.
   - The customer may request cancellation or rescheduling; clinic staff review
     the request before the original appointment is changed.
5. If required, the customer reviews and electronically signs the clinic
   contract.
6. The customer pays the required initial amount through the supported online
   payment flow.
7. Once the booking is approved, the contract is signed, and the initial
   payment is recorded, it can proceed to the service appointment.
8. At the appointment, the assigned practitioner verifies the customer service
   key before starting the service.
9. Starting the service records it as Ongoing and deducts any configured
   consumable materials from inventory.
10. The practitioner marks the service complete.
11. The customer confirms completion. If a balance remains, the booking is
    marked Balance Due; otherwise it becomes Completed.

For treatment plans with multiple sessions, completion of a session creates a
record and makes the next clinic-scheduled session ready.


9. ONLINE CONSULTATION PROCESS

Customer books an online consultation
-> clinic approves and assigns a practitioner
-> contract/payment requirements are completed
-> assigned practitioner creates Google Meet link
-> customer and practitioner join at the schedule
-> consultation is completed

Detailed steps:
1. The customer chooses a published Online Consultation and selects an
   available date/time.
2. The customer submits the consultation booking request.
3. Clinic staff approve/reject the request and ensure a practitioner is
   assigned.
4. The customer signs the consultation contract if the clinic requires one.
5. The customer pays the required consultation amount.
6. Only the assigned practitioner (or an authorized owner) can create the
   Google Meet link.
7. A meeting link can be created only after approval, required contract signing,
   and payment. Google Meet integration must also be configured for the system.
8. The customer sees the link in My Appointments; the practitioner sees it in
   Online Consultation. Both join at the scheduled time.
9. The practitioner completes the consultation and the customer sees its final
   status in appointment history.

An online consultation does not use the in-person service-key handoff. The
meeting link and appointment access control are the relevant controls.


10. SERVICE KEY PROCESS

The service key is a six-digit, one-time in-person service verification code.
It is not a payment code, POS code, or customer-order pickup code.

1. A booking is approved.
2. The customer signs the contract.
3. The system generates a six-digit service key for that appointment.
4. The customer makes the required initial payment.
5. The key is shown in the customer's My Appointments details. The customer
   shares it with the assigned practitioner in person.
6. The key is valid only from 30 minutes before the scheduled start until the
   end of the appointment.
7. Only the assigned practitioner can enter and verify the key.
8. A correct verified key allows the practitioner to start the service.
9. The system records the verification time as part of the appointment audit
   trail.

The customer should not need to separately confirm the same service key. The
customer's role is to present it; the assigned practitioner verifies it. This
prevents a staff member from starting an in-person service without the customer
being present.


11. PRODUCT, SERVICE, CONSULTATION, AND PACKAGE LISTING PROCESS

All customer-facing listings follow this approval path:

Create or edit draft
-> submit financial terms to Finance
-> Finance/owner approves or requests changes
-> owner/manager publishes approved listing
-> customers can see and use the published listing

COMMON LISTING STEPS
1. An authorized owner or manager opens Products & Services > Product &
   Service Listing and chooses Product, Service, Consultation, or Package.
2. They enter the required listing details, customer-facing description, image,
   price/payment terms, and terms and conditions.
3. The listing starts as Draft and is not visible to customers.
4. The owner or manager submits the financial terms for review.
5. Finance, or the clinic owner, reviews price, consultation fee, deposit or
   installment settings, discounts, and other payment terms.
   - Approve: listing becomes Finance Approved but remains unpublished.
   - Request changes: listing becomes Rejected/Changes Requested; the reason
     is saved and the owner/manager edits then resubmits it.
6. Only the owner or manager publishes the Finance-approved listing.
7. Only listings that are both Finance Approved and Published are visible to
   customers. They may later be unpublished or archived; an archived listing
   must be restored before it can be submitted again.

PRODUCT LISTING
1. Select an approved inventory product as the source.
2. Product details and availability are tied to the clinic's inventory.
3. Supplier-supplied FDA details are copied from inventory/supplier catalog and
   should be updated at the supplier/catalog source, not manually in the public
   listing.
4. Add the customer-facing description, product terms/return information, and
   image.
5. Submit, obtain Finance approval, and publish.
6. Customers can add the published product to cart, pay online, and collect it
   through pickup. Customer delivery is not supported.

SERVICE LISTING
1. Enter service name, description, duration, price, and service contract terms.
2. Configure booking rules, including eligible materials/resources, follow-up
   eligibility, and whether a consultation is required first.
3. If a consultation is required, choose whether it must be on-site or online
   and set the consultation fee.
4. Submit the financial terms, obtain approval, then publish.
5. Customers can request an appointment only after the service is published;
   applicable booking availability, practitioner, duration, and resource checks
   are enforced.

CONSULTATION LISTING
1. Enter consultation name, description, fee, duration, and terms.
2. Choose exactly one consultation mode:
   - On-site consultation, or
   - Online consultation.
3. Submit for Finance approval and publish after approval.
4. Customers can book it as a standalone consultation. An approved and
   completed consultation can also satisfy a service's consultation-first rule
   when it covers that service.

PACKAGE LISTING
1. Create the service and consultation listings that will be included first.
2. Create the package and select its included listings.
3. A valid package must contain at least one consultation and at least one
   service.
4. Set package price, contract terms, installment/deposit terms if allowed,
   and number of treatment sessions. Use one session for a one-time package.
5. Submit package financial terms for approval.
6. Before publishing the package, every included service/consultation must
   already be Finance Approved and Published.
7. Customers book and pay for the package as one appointment. For a
   multi-session package, the clinic schedules later sessions after each
   completed session.
